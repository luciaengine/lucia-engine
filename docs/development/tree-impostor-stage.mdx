# Tree impostor stage

## Question

Does an impostor last stage make far forests faster, or does cluster level of detail make it unnecessary?

Cluster level of detail does not help foliage. The cook refuses non-opaque materials (`Engine/Source/Assets/ClusterLOD/MeshClusterLODAsset.cpp`), and the runtime refines only opaque materials without vertex modifiers. Generated trees (`Packages/eztree`) have cutout leaves (`MaterialAlphaMode::Mask`) and a wind vertex modifier. HLOD does not help either: its member gather takes static `MeshRenderer` entities only (`Engine/Include/Assets/HlodMemberGather.h`), and the tree extraction removes the `MeshRenderer` from each tree.

## Current state of generated trees

- Each tree registers its own meshes. The key holds the entity id (`RuntimeGuidFromComponent` in `Packages/eztree/Native/EZTreeECS/Systems/EZTreeExtractionSystem.cpp`), so equal trees do not share a mesh.
- Each tree has its own bark and leaf materials. The extraction writes the wind parameters into them every frame.
- The tree meshes have no level of detail chain. The generator fills no `ExtraLODs`, and `MeshGPURegistry` uploads only the chain that the mesh carries.
- A default tree is about 25,000 triangles (65,928 bark indices and 9,216 leaf indices) and about 25.7 m wide and 25.3 m high.

## Measurement

Content: the editor default scene plus a grid of 32 x 32 generated trees, 15 m apart, default options. The camera is 600 m from the grid center, and the nearest tree is about 364 m away. Hardware: Apple M1 Max, macOS 15.7. Build: Release, Vulkan through MoltenVK, vsync off, a 1280x720 window, the default pipeline with cascaded shadows. Date: 2026-11-16. Each value is the median of 3 runs of 20 samples. The machine load was 3 to 12.

| Arm | GPU ms | CPU frame ms | Draw slots |
|---|---|---|---|
| No trees | 1.2 | 2.0 | 6 |
| Trees as they are now | 28.5 | 45.3 | 12294 |
| One seed, own mesh and materials per tree | 28.7 | 45.4 | 12294 |
| One seed, one shared mesh and material set | 25.5 | 26.8 | 18 |
| One tree-sized cutout quad per tree, shared | 4.7 | 5.3 | 12 |

The shared arm used a scratch switch that keys the mesh and the materials by the options hash. The quad arm is the floor of an impostor: it has no texture fetches and casts no cascade shadows. A real impostor costs more than the floor.

Diagnostic runs on a loaded machine (load 90 to 190, so the times show direction only) give these results:

- Fewer bark triangles (2.9x fewer per tree) remove 2% of the tree cost.
- Wind off does not change the GPU time.
- A window with 4x fewer pixels does not make the main pass cheaper.
- At 1024 trees, the main pass takes about 22 ms and each cascade takes less than 1 ms.
- On the CPU, render graph execution takes 38 ms of a 49 ms frame. That time is the recording of 12,294 draw slots.

## Decision rule and result

The rule was written before the runs: build the stage only if the impostor floor removes at least 30% of the GPU time of the cheapest competitor, and the gain is larger than 3 times the control spread.

- Against trees as they are now: (28.5 - 4.7) / 28.5 = 83%.
- Against shared variants, the decisive competitor: (25.5 - 4.7) / 25.5 = 81%. The control spread is 0.2 ms.

Shared variants cut the CPU frame time by 41% and the draw slots from 12,294 to 18, but cut the GPU time by only 11%. The far-forest GPU cost is the full tree geometry itself. An impostor removes it. Both steps are necessary: shared variants for the CPU, and impostors for the GPU.

The main pass cost is not attributed to a single cause. Triangle count, wind, pixel count and draw count are ruled out. A hypothesis, not checked, is overdraw of cutout leaves. The impostor removes the cost whatever the cause.

## Owner decision

Verbatim: "use hemi octa for trees, both modes in the bake". The bake supports hemi-octahedral and full octahedral atlases. Hemi-octahedral is the default for trees and foliage. Full octahedral is for objects that the camera sees from all sides, as in flight and space games.

## Design

### Step 1: shared tree variants

The tree package owns this step.

- Key the generated meshes and materials by the options hash, with a reference count per variant. The last tree that releases a variant unregisters its meshes.
- Move the per-tree wind variation out of the material. The material holds the variant's wind parameters. The vertex modifier derives a per-tree phase from the instance position, so equal trees do not sway in step.
- Keep the wind volume response per tree through the instance data, or resolve it per variant and region. The first change measures which one keeps the look.
- Acceptance: the 32 x 32 forest scene shows about 18 draw slots and a CPU frame time within 10% of the shared arm above, with the same look at a near pose.

### Step 2: impostor bake

The asset system owns the bake, beside the mesh level of detail cache and the HLOD baker. The renderer draws it.

- The input is a mesh and its materials. The output is an impostor atlas of N x N frames, with base color and alpha, plus normal and depth.
- Two layouts, in `Mathematics/Octahedral.h` with unit tests: hemi-octahedral around +Y (the default for trees and foliage) and full octahedral around +Z (the full sphere). The shaders use the same encodings (`GE_HemiOctEncode`, `GE_OctEncode` in `Includes/octahedral_normal.glsl`).
- The bake draws each frame through the render graph with an orthographic camera fitted to the bounds. A pass keyword, `ImpostorBake`, gives the forward adapter a one-target shape like `MotionVectors`: it runs the material's own surface and alpha discard and writes base color, alpha, normal and depth, with no lighting. So every material bakes through its own shader, the custom leaf surface and authored bark materials included.
- The bake draws the rest pose: it uses the static twin materials, which have no wind modifier.
- The bake spreads its frames over several rendered frames. The mesh draws until the atlas is ready. A device rebuild bakes again.
- The atlas is cached by the mesh content hash, the material hash, the layout and the resolution.

**Atlas memory.** An atlas of 8 x 8 frames at 128 pixels, one RGBA8 and one RGBA16F texture, is 12 MB. A forest whose every tree has its own seed has one variant per tree, so a bake for every variant does not fit. The rules:

- A variant bakes on demand, when one of its instances first reaches impostor distance.
- A memory cap holds the atlases. Past the cap, the least recently drawn atlas goes, and its trees draw the mesh.
- Shared variants come first for this reason too: a forest that reuses 8 to 16 tree variants needs 8 to 16 atlases.

### Step 3: runtime last stage

The first runtime slice is built in the tree package, which owns the trees:

- The tree extraction measures each tree's screen coverage from the viewpoint camera with the HLOD metric (`Hlod::ClusterCoverage`, `Hlod::UpdateProxyActive`) on the impostor sphere. Below the switch coverage (one atlas texel per screen pixel) it draws one card instance with the variant's impostor material instead of the bark and leaf parts. A tree keeps its mesh until its atlas is baked and its card pipeline is built.
- The card material uses the engine's `Surfaces/impostor.glsl` and `VertexModifiers/impostor_card.glsl`. The CPU writes the instance's `custom0`: the hemi-octahedral code of the view direction in the tree's frame, and the tree's yaw for the normals. The card faces the view direction and blends the 3 frames around it. The depth of the dominant frame gives the surface point under each card pixel, and the other frames sample that point; a frame that has a surface clearly in front of it sees an occluder there and does not contribute. The atlas texels outside the tree are 0, so a filtered texel is premultiplied by its coverage. The frames sit on the grid points from edge to edge, so the outer frames look from the horizon.
- Cards cast cascade shadows, but in shadow passes they face the camera, not the light. Step 4 replaces this slice.

### Step 4: general mesh stage

Any static mesh gets the stage, and the selection runs on the GPU. The tree package uses the same path, so the engine has one implementation.

**The group.** A `MeshRenderer` holds one submesh and one material, so a model with bark and leaves is two entities and two instances. A card must replace all parts of one object. The group is one model asset:

- The opt-in is the model setting `assets.model.impostor` (default off), shown in the model inspector. It is for trees, large foliage and props that are seen from far away. A small mesh with few triangles (grass, a fern) does not pay: its card costs three atlas samples per pixel.
- The bake draws all submeshes of the model, each with the material of the first entity that draws it. An entity that overrides the material of a linked part still gets that card.
- Each part of a linked model uses the whole model's box as its `LocalBounds`. So every part has the same culling sphere and the same coverage, and every part makes the same switch decision in the same frame. No frame shows a card with a mesh part, or a hole. The mesh LOD of a part then reads a slightly larger sphere and keeps detail a little longer.

**The link.** `GPUMesh` word 20 (`impostorCard`) holds the link of a submesh: 0 for none, otherwise the card material's GPU-scene index plus 1 in bits 0 to 23, and bit 31 for the primary part. Word 44 (`nextImpostorPart`) closes the parts of one model into a circular list, and word 48 (`impostorSwitchCoverage`) holds the model's own switch coverage (0 for the view's default). The registry wrote the vertex bucket and index type into words 44 and 48, which no reader used. `GPUScene::SetMeshImpostorGroup` links all parts of a model or none, and a mesh row update from the mesh registry keeps the words. Submesh 0 is the primary part and draws the card. The other parts draw nothing past the switch.

**The card row.** `BatchRegistry` counts each instance of a primary linked mesh in the card row (card material, card quad mesh) as well as in its own row. Every table builder reads the registry, so the plain, shadow-class, color-class and pool-group tables all hold the card row, with a capacity for every instance that can switch.

**The selection.** In `draw_command_scatter.comp`, before the batch search, a linked instance measures its coverage on its instance sphere, with the HLOD metric of the tree package (radius * proj[1][1] / distance, the LOD bias folded in). The default enter coverage is one atlas frame across the render target (128 pixels / render height, 0.1185 at 1080 lines), so the switch keeps one atlas texel per pixel at every resolution. A model can set its own screen height (**Impostor Screen Height (%)**). The card stays until the coverage is 1.15 times the enter coverage. Below the switch the card replaces every mesh LOD of the parts. A card frame shows at most one atlas texel per pixel there, while a coarse LOD of alpha-masked foliage loses its leaves: on the Megascans tree the leaf LOD chain reaches an error of 31% of the mesh extent at LOD 2 and 83% at LOD 3, and in the canopy and ridge scenes the trees show bare branches at mid range with the stage off. A rule that waited for the last mesh LOD (LOD 3 starts at about 540 m there) never drew a card inside the scene.

- A model that must show its last mesh LOD before the card sets its own **Impostor Screen Height (%)** below the default. At 1080 lines the default is 11.85% of the screen height.
- **The placement owner.** All parts of one placed object read one switch state. `MeshImpostorSystem` groups the renderers of a model by a hash of their world position and basis, and the instance of part 0 owns every part of a complete placement. `GPUScene` keeps one owner word per instance (`impostorOwner`, scatter binding 14, `0xFFFFFFFF` for none). The scatter accepts the owner only when it is a live instance, visible in this slice with the same world key, the primary part of the same card, and has the same sphere and LOD bias. Otherwise the part draws its mesh. A part without an instance marks the scan as pending, and the system scans again every 16 updates. The tree package sets the owners of each tree's parts.
- Camera slices read the hysteresis state from the rendered history of the owner (level 255 is a card). Every part of a placement therefore reads the same history and switches in the same frame. The mesh LOD history (`prevLod`) does not hold card state.
- Cascade slices select with the view camera (they already do for mesh LOD) and have no history. They switch at the enter coverage, so inside the hysteresis band a far tree can cast the mesh shadow while it draws the card, or the reverse.
- Slices that force a level (virtual shadow pages, editor 2D views) never draw cards.
- A card frame writes no crossfade pair and records level 255 in the rendered history, so a motion consumer sees the switch as a break.

**The card vertex stage.** The card draws with the mesh instance's own `GPUInstance`. A vertex modifier may define `ModifyInstance`, which the adapter runs before `ModifyVertex`. The impostor card modifier computes `custom0` there from the pass's viewer and the instance transform: the octahedral code of the view direction in the object's frame and the cosine and sine of its yaw. The surface shader reads `custom0` as before. The viewer comes from the rebased view-projection that the pass projects with (`Cam.uVPRel`): the eye of a perspective pass, and the direction toward the light of an orthographic pass. So a cascade draws the card that the light sees, which makes the shadow cards face the light. A tilted instance gets card normals that turn with the yaw only.

**The card light.** A card is flat, so without help the sun lights the whole crown like its outer leaves. A surface that declares `// @primaryLightOcclusion` gets the material keyword `SurfaceLightOcclusion` and the define `GE_SURFACE_LIGHT_OCCLUSION`. The impostor surface then finds the shaded point in the atlas frames near the light direction and compares depths (`GE_ImpostorCrownLight`), so the inner crown gets less sun. A shape that shades nothing (depth-only passes) drops the define.

**The card passes.** The card reads its coverage from the three frames at the card point, at the shading mip. Every pass reads the same coverage, so the depth prepass and the color pass keep the same pixels, and the depth-only passes (the prepass and the shadow cascades) stop after three atlas reads. The color pass reuses those reads for the surface point, then samples the frames at that point. A card modifier declares `// @deformationClock none`, so the card stays out of the deformation motion pass: both motion endpoints give the same position, and the depth reprojection already gives its motion.

**The sun on the card.** The blend of the frames' leaf normals is shorter than 1 where the leaves face different ways. A pixel of the mesh averages the sun over those leaves, so the card scales its sun by that length.

**The bake sampling.** The bake variant samples material textures 2 mips finer (`GE_MATERIAL_MIP_BIAS`, `kImpostorBakeMipBias` = -2). One atlas frame holds the whole object, so at the normal mip a leaf texel merges with its gaps and the alpha test drops the leaf. A bias of 0 gives bare cards. A bias of -8 gives pale cards.

**The bake.** An engine system finds the opted-in models in the world, requests their atlas from `ImpostorBaker` every frame (which keeps the atlas in the least-recently-used budget), registers the card material, and sets the links once the card pipeline is built. An evicted atlas clears the links, and the parts draw their meshes again.

Not in this step, and the next steps for the stage:

- The dither crossfade between mesh and card (`Includes/lod_crossfade.glsl`).
- Depth from the atlas.

### Verification

- Unit tests: the octahedral math, the frame cameras of the bake, the atlas cache key, and scatter selection at the threshold. A GPU bake does not give the same bytes on every backend, so a capture checks the atlas pixels instead of a hash test.
- Performance: a 32 x 32 forest of 16 tree variants at the far pose, real impostors against shared variants, 3 runs on a quiet machine. One variant and 1024 variants are the two ends, measured for the record, not for the gate. Acceptance: at least 30% less GPU time than shared variants.
- Look: a capture pair at the transition distance and at the far pose, at a game camera height and at a flight camera height.
